The problem

Wave data stops offshore. The questions are at the coast.

Global hindcasts give hourly waves for decades, but on cells of ~50 km (10 km at best) in deep water. Near the coast, depth, headlands and sandbanks reshape every wave within a few hundred metres.

The key trick

Each goal on its own, then add them up

Each offshore goal is learned on its own. At the coast, their waves are simply added together.

  • A swell and a wind sea from different goals both reach the coast.
  • A few hundred runs per goal are enough.
  • Checked against full wave-model runs and buoys.

Optional · local wind

Wind on top, as one more contribution

The training runs have no wind: they carry the waves in from offshore. Where the wind keeps blowing over a shallow shelf, the sea at the coast is bigger, so we add that energy back.

  • In every hour, find the wind sea: the short, steep waves the local wind still grows.
  • K says how much bigger it gets with the wind, from wind speed, fetch, depth and angle. Learned once, from ~100 runs with and without wind.
  • One switch: the goal sum stays the same, the wind is added after it.

Being honest

Where it struggles, and what's next

A fast surrogate inherits the limits of its physics and its forcing. These are the current weak spots.

Known limits

  • Mean direction against buoys: no clear gain yet over the offshore hindcast on average, even though it closely follows the full simulation.
  • Hour by hour and a linear sum: no travel time across the domain, and breaking sees one goal at a time. The cost shows at very shallow, sheltered sites (5 m, inside inlets, behind reefs).
  • It can only be as good as the offshore hindcast, and in open water downscaling adds little. Calibrating the hindcast against satellite altimetry removes most of its bias, but over-corrects moderate seas (open).

Next

  • More regions in HyGloW, contributed by other teams through the same case contract.
  • Beyond hindcasts: forecasts and climate projections through the same trained HyWaves metamodels, with no new physics runs.
  • Nearshore waves as input to impact models: run-up, coastal flooding, erosion.
  • A public deployment of the Results Explorer.

The Ecosystem

BlueMath — open-source ocean science

An open-source Python ecosystem for hybrid numerical + statistical methods. BinWaves and HyWaves, two wave downscaling methods, both live here.

  • Born September 2020, from a NOAA-led international workshop (Spain, USA, Australia, Fiji, New Zealand) held during COVID — now a genuine community model library, grown to TU Delft, UC Santa Cruz, UFSC Brazil, ERDC, IFCA and more.
  • Toolkit (BlueMath-tk) — data-mining/statistical models + numerical model wrappers — Methods (hybrid/statistical/dynamical downscaling) — Climate Services (hindcast, forecast, probabilistic assessment, climate change).
  • Cross-platform, cloud-ready: quick tests in DevContainers, heavy runs on HPC — the setup HyGloW runs on.
  • Showcased use cases: MUSCLE, HyWindSea, HySwash, GreenSurge 2.0, and for nearshore waves BinWaves and HyWaves.

Write the model input once with Jinja placeholders. The wrapper creates a named folder for every case, runs them on a laptop, in a container or on HPC, and returns everything as one xarray Dataset.

Training design

Each goal samples only the directions that reach the coast

Goal points sit on the offshore boundary. Each one only samples directions that can actually reach the coast from it, so no runs are spent on waves travelling out to sea.

The output

Swells and wind sea, transformed into directional spectra

Real sea states mix swells from different storms and local wind sea. HyWaves propagates each component on its own, then adds them up at the coast.

  • The offshore hindcast (WHACS) splits each hour into up to 4 partitions: one wind sea plus three swells.
  • Each partition goes through the metamodel on its own, as a simple sea state from one direction.
  • The nearshore spectrum is the linear sum of the transformed partitions, so two swells arriving from different directions still appear as two peaks at the coast.
  • A cheaper bulk mode (one sea state per hour) is also available.
